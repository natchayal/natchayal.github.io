Professional Summary

Ph.D. Candidate, Civil & Environmental Engineering, University of South Florida.

Natchaya's career embodies interdisciplinary research and a commitment to advancing drinking water treatment, policy, and sustainable infrastructure. As a Ph.D. candidate in Civil Engineering, she brings more than five years of combined research and professional experience and over ten years of laboratory experience across academic, research, and professional settings. She integrates behavioral decision-making, participatory research, regulatory analysis, and physicochemical and biological treatment processes to address complex water-quality challenges. Her work spans the fate and transport of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), 1,4-Dioxane, EPA regulatory-data analysis, potable reuse optimization, and biofiltration for emerging contaminant removal. She has authored more than five peer-reviewed publications, delivered over ten presentations at national conferences, and advanced the water sector through teaching, mentorship, technical workshops, and leadership in the American Water Works Association.

Research Interest

Drinking water treatment; PFAS and emerging contaminants; biofiltration; potable reuse; environmental policy and regulatory analysis; data-driven treatment optimization; microbial community dynamics; and applications of machine learning and agent-based modeling to biological filtration systems.

Career Endeavor

Natchaya aims to advance data-driven tools and sustainable drinking water treatment methods to improve water quality and support informed regulatory decision-making in the United States. Through research on emerging contaminants, biofiltration, potable reuse, treatment-process optimization, and risk communication, she aims to develop practical methods, models, and decision-support tools that help water utilities improve treatment performance, comply with evolving regulations, and protect public health. Her work will translate scientific and regulatory data into actionable strategies that strengthen public trust and improve the safety and resilience of drinking water systems in the U.S. and globally.
